## La Société Guernesiaise Astronomy Section

I grew up in Guernsey, and most of my early outreach was through the island's [astronomical society](http://www.astronomy.org.gg/). I have held several positions on its council, including Editor of the newsletter. Its members include retired professional astronomers, and they are a large part of why I chose astronomy as a career. One of them, [David Le Conte](https://www.astronomy.org.gg/more/articles/dleconte-obituary), nominated me for Fellowship of the Royal Astronomical Society. He took the only photograph of the Apollo 10 trans-lunar injection burn, and was Executive Director of the Smithsonian Institution's Research Foundation in Washington DC.

### At the observatory

- Operated the section's telescopes, including the 16-inch Meade LX200, at weekly public open evenings over several summers, showing visitors the Moon, the planets and the night sky.
- Took solar telescopes into schools to show sixth-form students sunspots and explain the solar cycle ([Guernsey Press article](https://guernseypress.com/news/2020/07/10/out-in-the-sun-all-in-the-name-of-science/)).
- Wrote articles and activities for children on black holes, galaxies and nebulae for the section's website.
- Wrote and edited the twice-yearly [Sagittarius newsletter](http://www.astronomy.org.gg/more/newsletters), which goes to the membership and to local schools and includes articles by local students.
